# Flat screen TVs

Status: implemented (draft PR: the TVs and surround speakers on their own, on upstream main); played in game on Justin's PC (r132 to r145), some parts not yet (see Verification)
Proposal issue: Justin's request, 26 October 2026 ("Flatscreen TVs"); the mockups r22b/tv, flat_tv_take1
Owner: Justin (owner); built by Claude
Target milestone and tier: the pre-made plants' building kit (machines feature), any tier
Primary specialty and supported player role: decoration and shared viewing for any player

## Player experience
- **A block of screen.** The Flat Screen TV is one block of a modern flat panel, 5 cm deep. Place blocks side by side and one above another on the same wall and they join into one screen, up to **48 x 27** blocks (8 x 5 until 27 October 2026; Justin: "as tall or as wide as you want"). A bigger screen is heard further: 24 blocks out from its middle plus half its longer side. The bezel is only round the outside. The chin along the bottom carries the NARV-TECH logo and the power light beside it: **blue on, red off**. A longer row, or an odd shape, splits into the fewest screens up to 48 x 27 (`tv/TvLayout.java`: from the bottom left, as far right as it goes, then as far up as every column goes).
- **Hung or standing.** With a wall behind it (anything solid behind any of its blocks) the screen hangs flat on the wall. With nothing behind it, the panel stands in the middle of its block on a neck and a foot, lifted 10 cm, with the electronics housing on its back. Change what's behind and it changes.
- **The SCREEN window.** Right-click any block of a screen. With an empty hand or a block in it, that works from up to **48 blocks** away (a big screen is watched from well back; the game's own reach is 5 blocks): a right-click that reaches nothing in the game's reach looks on to the first thing in the way, and if that's a TV its window opens (`client/tv/TvFarUse.java`, `mixin/client/TvFarUseMixin`; the server checks the distance too). Sneak, or hold anything else, and the right-click is the game's own. The window stays open while you're within 56 blocks of the screen's nearest block. (Fixed 27 October 2026: on a big screen the window measured from the screen's bottom-left corner, so it shut as soon as it opened when you clicked the far end.)
  - **ON THE SCREEN:** the picture, live.
  - **NOW PLAYING:** the video's title, where it comes from (YouTube or the site), its size and frame rate, and **WATCHING:** how many players are within hearing.
  - The **seek bar** with the time and the length; **◀◀ 10s**, **PAUSE / PLAY**, **10s ▶▶**, **STOP**; the **volume**. The time and the bar show what's on the screen: while the picture catches up after a seek or buffers, the picture's own time (it doesn't run on while nothing plays).
  - **VIDEO LINK:** paste a YouTube link, or the web address of a video file (MP4, WebM, or anything FFmpeg reads over http(s)), and press **PLAY LINK** (or Enter).
  - **REPLAY WHEN IT ENDS:** ticked, the video (or the playlist) starts again from the beginning when it ends, for everyone.
  - **PLAY WHOLE PLAYLIST:** greyed unless the link (playing, or typed in) is a YouTube playlist link (`list=`). Ticked, the screen plays the playlist's videos one after another from the one the link names; NOW PLAYING says **VIDEO n OF m** and the playlist's name, and the seek bar is that video's. Unticked, only the video the link names plays. (Justin, 27 October 2026.)
- **It streams; nothing is downloaded** but the helpers below. The picture and sound come straight from YouTube (or the site) a few seconds ahead, as a browser plays them.
- **Long videos:** for YouTube the screen asks first for the HLS streams (the video in short pieces, any length plays). When YouTube refuses a stream it found, it tries the next way (`TvHelpers.WAYS`): the big DASH files again, fetched through **the relay** (`client/tv/TvRelay.java`: FFmpeg reads from this game's own 127.0.0.1 address, a random link only it knows, and the relay asks YouTube for 10 MB at a time with the headers yt-dlp gives, as yt-dlp itself downloads them, and answers FFmpeg's jumps as byte ranges; the stream's address is then asked for over IPv4, as Java connects), then as YouTube's Vision Pro app, the Android app (its 360p stream), the embedded player. The way that worked is tried first next time. What yt-dlp found is kept for 30 minutes, so a screen walked away from and back to starts at once.
- **Why a video wouldn't play (27 October 2026, `youtu.be/jJ9vtKtilG8`, 2 h 27 min):** YouTube gave no HLS streams for it, and every big stream yt-dlp found (as YouTube's TV app, 720p and 360p) answered HTTP 403 Forbidden when FFmpeg opened it in one request (FFmpeg's error -858797304 is its code for a 403). The screen now says "YouTube refused the video's streams (HTTP 403)" instead of the stream's address, and tries the ways above before it gives up.
- **Jumping in a long video (fixed 27 October 2026).** A seek to 5:30 in that 2.5-hour video froze the picture for good. FFmpeg's seek lands on the key frame before the time asked (YouTube's are a few seconds apart), and the reader threw those frames away as "behind" and jumped again, for ever. Now a jump goes a little past the screen's moment (as far as a jump has been taking on this computer, learned as it goes), and the picture's reader decodes on from the key frame without drawing (several times faster than playing) up to the moment due; it never jumps again for being behind a key frame. Measured on a stand-in stream (key frames 5 s apart, seeks taking 3 to 6 s): one seek per jump, the picture in step within 20 ms once it's there.
- **A seek holds the screen until the picture is there (27 October 2026).** A seek while it plays (the 10s buttons, the bar) holds the screen at the new moment, the clock stopped, for everyone (the window says SEEKING), until the game of the player who moved it has the picture there; then it plays on from exactly there (or after 60 seconds, from another game of the screen's party, if the mover's never gets there). Before, the screen's clock ran on while the stream caught up, so the timer moved with nothing playing and the picture came back further on. Paused, a seek keeps it paused at the new moment. A short way on (up to 25 seconds: one or two presses of 10s ▶▶) asks the stream for nothing new: the readers decode on through it from where they are. Measured on a stand-in stream: 10s ▶▶ in place in 0.7 s with no seek; two presses in 1 s; a jump to 20:00 one seek of 3 s, then on from exactly 20:00.
- **The pause menu doesn't stop the sound (27 October 2026, r144).** In single player, Escape held the TV's sound while its picture went on with the screen's clock, and the sound came back behind it (for good on a live stream, which had nothing to catch it up). Now only the screen's own pause holds the sound; and a live stream's sound, like a video's, starts lined up with its picture and is started again where the picture is when it drifts more than 0.6 s (a video's 0.25 s).
- **A live stream's sound in step with its picture (27 October 2026, r146).** Justin, after r145: "The audio is still off a good bit on the kick stream". Three things, found in a simulation of the sound against a stand-in live stream and sound card (scratchpad `sndsim/`):
  - Starting (and after every hiccup), a live stream's sound took the first chunk it had even when that chunk was up to 0.6 s old, so it played up to 0.6 s behind the picture, and nothing set it right until it was further off than that. Now it starts at the picture's clock, as a video's does.
  - Set again where the clock is when it's off by more than 0.12 s (it was 0.6 s live, 0.25 s a video), from what it had queued, with no gap (it used to drop 1.4 s of queued sound and wait). Off on average more than 35 ms, it's played up to 0.5% slower or faster until within 10 ms (the speakers alike), so a sound card's clock that isn't quite the game's doesn't walk it off. Measured: within 32 ms of the picture with a sound card 0.3% fast, 56 ms 0.3% slow (it was up to 110 ms, then a jump).
  - A live picture more than 0.1 s behind its clock (a stall, or a computer decoding it slower than it plays) is decoded on without being shown until it's back: before, it stayed behind for good while the sound kept to the clock.
  - Every 30 s a live stream's line-up goes in the log (`live line-up: sound ... from the picture`), to see what a stream does on a player's PC.
- **Rumble and Kick (27 October 2026, r143).** A Rumble video or live stream (`rumble.com/v...`) and a Kick live stream or video (`kick.com/<channel>`, its videos) play as YouTube's do: through yt-dlp, in step for everyone, with jumps. Checked first on Justin's PC with `22-tv-sites-test.bat` (`tv-jump-test/TvSiteTest.java`, results in `tv-site-rumble.txt` and `tv-site-kick.txt`):
  - Rumble's pages turn yt-dlp away (HTTP 403, its bot check) unless it asks as Chrome does (`--impersonate chrome`; the Windows yt-dlp has curl_cffi built in); its video servers then answer anyone. Its streams are HLS with the sound in them (360p to 1080p); every piece answered at once, 2 h 45 min and 4 h 8 min into a 4.6-hour episode. It names its pieces `.tar`, which FFmpeg 7+ refuses unless told any name will do (`allowed_segment_extensions ALL`, `extension_picky 0`; only web protocols are allowed under it anyway).
  - Kick answers yt-dlp plainly; its live HLS (to 1080p60, sound in it) opened in a third of a second.
  - Ways (`TvHelpers.Site`): Rumble as Chrome, as Safari, plainly; Kick plainly, as Chrome; each at most 720p HLS, read straight by FFmpeg. The window names the site.
- **Timestamped links start there (27 October 2026, r143).** A YouTube link's `t=` (2656, 90s, 1h2m3s) or `start=`, or a Rumble link's `start=` (seconds), sets where the new link is held when played (`TvBlockEntity.linkStartMs`): it loads there and starts for everyone from there. Other links' query strings aren't read (a stream address's `t=` is often a cache-breaking clock).
- **Full screen (27 October 2026, r143).** Sneak and right-click a screen that's on, with an empty hand, from as far as 25 blocks (near too): its video fills the game window (`TvFullScreen`), the picture this game already plays for it (in step with everyone), letterboxed on black, its sound heard as if sitting in front of it wherever the player stands. Moving the mouse brings up the title, the time and how far along for 3 seconds; Escape or a right-click leaves; it closes by itself when the screen is turned off. Sneaking with a block in hand is left to the game (building a screen out).
- **Jumping far into a YouTube video (27 October 2026, evening; r142).** Justin jumped 33:39, then 44:16, into the 2.5-hour video: SEEKING TO, then RECONNECTING for minutes, the clock running on with nothing playing. The first fix (r141) guessed at YouTube's HLS stream; Justin then ran `21-tv-jump-test.bat` (jugcraft-test; `tv-jump-test/TvJumpTest.java`, results in `tv-jump-test.txt`, no addresses in it), which asks YouTube for the video every way the TV can and tries pieces deep in. What it showed:
  - YouTube no longer gives a 720p HLS stream with its sound in it, only the picture (itag 232) and the sound (233/234) apart. The TV's "hls" way asked for the one with sound, didn't find it, and fell through to the big files (136 + 251), which FFmpeg read straight from YouTube.
  - A big file asked for "everything from here on" partway in gets no answer at all from YouTube (40 s, nothing), where a bounded 10 MB piece comes in a fifth of a second. A jump in a big file read straight is just such a request: that was the hang.
  - The HLS pieces answer at once at any depth (44 minutes, 2 hours in), and FFmpeg jumped 44 minutes into the HLS picture in 0.1 s.
  - A stream YouTube has only just made out can be refused (403) for a second or two, then answers as any other.
  Now:
  - the first way asks for YouTube's HLS picture and HLS sound as a pair (`TvHelpers.HLS`), read by FFmpeg straight (no `--force-ipv4`: FFmpeg and yt-dlp then reach YouTube the same way; Justin's PC has IPv6). A jump in them is a seek in the stream, as before r141 (r141's "open it anew" for HLS is gone);
  - every other way asks only for big files, always read through the relay in bounded pieces;
  - the relay asks a refused first piece again up to four times, a second apart, and the player opens a refused stream once more after 2 s before trying another way;
  - a jump that still stays stuck 25 s starts the player over another way, saying SEEKING TO, held at the new moment;
  - while this game's picture is stuck (started over, or buffering 3 s), it holds the screen's clock at the last picture it showed and starts it again once the picture's back (`TvPlayers.hold`; the window's chip says RECONNECTING or BUFFERING; at most once in 10 s; the server takes it only from the screen's party). The clock no longer runs on with nothing playing.
  Checked on a stand-in stream (tvsim4): a jump to 44:16 is one seek and in place in 0.12 s; a 10 s skip decodes on with no seek; a stalled stream shows BUFFERING with the last picture's time to hold at; a jump that never answers breaks the player at 25 s.
- **Making a screen bigger doesn't open the video again (27 October 2026).** Blocks added to a screen's left or below move its bottom-left block, and each game used to open the video afresh for every block added: building out a 23-wide screen opened Justin's video 27 times in a minute, after which YouTube refused it (HTTP 403) and the next seek hung until the screen started over. Now the block's link count goes with the screen (`TvBlockEntity.Snapshot.session`) and each game moves the screen's player to the new bottom-left block and plays on (`TvPlayers.moved`); only growing past two blocks opens it again once, for the sharper picture.
- **The screen says what holds it up.** Over the picture, on a dark band low down: **SEEKING TO 20:00** (with the seconds it has taken, and "waiting on the stream" after 8), **BUFFERING** (the picture over 0.8 s behind with nothing new: the stream isn't keeping up), **STREAM STOPPED: why** (red). The TV's window shows the same on its info line.
- **It starts over by itself.** A stream that stops mid-way (a lapsed YouTube link, a dropped connection) or stays stuck for 25 s is found again and goes on from the screen's moment, saying RECONNECTING; up to 3 times in 2 minutes, then it says why it stopped.
- **PLAY LINK starts over.** Pressing it, even with the same link, starts the screen over for everyone: each game lets its player go and finds the streams afresh, from the start (the block counts the links played, `TvBlockEntity.session`).
- **"Could not open input" after playing while it loaded (27 October 2026):** that was YouTube's 403 again (the log shows every stream refused at 06:37), not the click; the build then had no other ways to ask. Playing while it loads now starts it where the screen is, through the jump above.
- **Paused, the picture holds (fixed 27 October 2026, after the seek fix):** paused, the screen kept taking the next picture as "the paused one", so the picture ran on while the sound stopped, and on PLAY it was minutes ahead of the sound and waited. Paused, it now shows one picture where the screen is paused (the first after a jump) and nothing more; a reader that has run on ahead of the screen with no jump in hand jumps back.
- **A new link always starts (fixed 27 October 2026):** a new link is held at its start until the game of the player who set it has the picture. Two things left it held for good (LOADING in the corner, 00:00, no sound; only breaking the TV cleared it): this game forgot it had set the link the moment it pressed PLAY LINK (the block's update hadn't come back yet), and a link still held when the game or the server stopped was held again after it started, with nobody to start it. Now a game that set the link starts it for two minutes after, any other game with the picture ready starts it once it has been held 60 seconds (10, then 15, before; the server lets only the screen's party), and a link held when the world was saved starts as it loads.
- **Playlists, in step for everyone:** yt-dlp lists the playlist without opening each video (at most 300; private or removed ones are left out). The screen's clock runs through the list by the lengths YouTube gives, so every game works out the same video and the same moment in it. The next video is found 30 seconds before it's due and opened, paused at its start, 8 seconds before, so it follows on at once (`client/tv/TvProgram.java`, `TvPlayers`).
- **Everyone sees the same moment.** The server keeps only the link, its own clock when the video was at its start, and whether and where it is paused. Each player's game plays the video itself and works out where it should be from that. A player who walks up late joins in where it is, and a seek or a pause reaches everyone.
- **A new link waits for its setter.** It shows LOADING for everyone, held at 0. It starts for all once the game of the player who set it has the first picture (or, after 60 seconds, anyone else's of the screen's party).
- **Sound comes from the screen.** It is mono, from the middle of the glass, fading evenly to nothing at **24 blocks**. It follows the screen's volume and the player's Jukebox/Note Blocks and Master sliders. It goes on under the pause menu, as the picture does (r144).
- **Walls muffle it**, as they do the game's own sounds (`client/SoundOcclusion`, the diesel generator's system): a wall lets about a tenth through with almost none of its high end, two walls stop it, a closed door counts nearly as a wall, glass a little less. Worked out ten times a second and eased, through the TV's own OpenAL low-pass filter (Justin, 27 October 2026: "not everybody hears your YouTube stuff").
- **Surround speakers (27 October 2026, r145).** Justin: "add in speakers ... two blocks tall, a speaker on top of a thin stand ... realistic ... automatically syncs with the nearest screen ... a sync key ... so that people can create a surround sound system". Approved from the mockup sheet (`tools/tv_speaker.py`, scratchpad `spk/`).
  - **The Surround Speaker** (`tv/TvSpeakerBlock.java`): a NARV-TECH SR-6 on its stand, two blocks tall (1.97 m). A walnut two-way box (a 6.5 in woven woofer, a 1 in silk dome tweeter in a waveguide, a slot port, the status light) on a black anodised column with its cord down inside, a steel base plate on four spikes in floor cups, the cord into a floor outlet. It turns sixteen ways (as a sign) to face the player who places it. Breaking either half takes both; the lower half drops it. Crafted (two): planks, a note block, a copper ingot and an iron ingot (`PNP / PCP / _I_`).
  - **Linking:** set down, it links itself to the nearest screen within **24 blocks** and says which (`TvSpeakerBlockEntity.nearestScreen`). Right-clicked with an empty hand it says which screen it plays.
  - **The Sync Key** (`tv/SyncKeyItem.java`; shapeless: an iron nugget, redstone, a copper ingot) links speakers by hand, for screens close together. Right-click a screen, then speakers, to link each to it; or a speaker, then a screen. Sneak and right-click a speaker to unlink it; sneak and right-click the air to clear the key. A speaker links to a screen in its own dimension within **32 blocks**. The key shines while it's set, and says what to in its tooltip.
  - **Its sound:** each player's game plays a linked screen's sound from every speaker on it too, as well as from the screen (`client/tv/TvPlayer` voices, `TvSpeakers`). Each speaker has its own OpenAL source and buffers, fed the same sound, started, paused and stopped with the screen's at the same moment; one more than 40 ms out of step is set again from the screen's queue at the sample it's playing. A speaker is heard to **16 blocks**, loudest in front (about half behind it), muffled by walls as the screen is; at most 8 a screen. Full screen, the speakers go on playing where they are.
  - **What's live** (`client/TvSpeakerRenderer`): the woofer's cone and surround move out and in with the low end of the sound as this game plays it (at most 1.8 mm); the status light on the front is white while its screen's sound plays, dim while linked and quiet, amber unlinked; the LINK light on the back is cyan linked, off unlinked; both blink cyan for two seconds after a link is made.
  - Nothing runs on the server but the link (kept on the lower half, sent to the players' games).
- **Who can change it:** the placer's party (or anyone, for a screen nobody placed: the pre-made admin building's). Players in creative can too. Everyone can watch. The window says whose party it is and greys the controls for everyone else.

## Balance and automation
- Cosmetic: no power and no light level of its own (the picture is drawn full-bright).
- Server cost: none while playing. The server stores a few fields on the screen's bottom-left block. It works only when a player uses a control, or when blocks are placed or broken beside it: it joins up at most 400 blocks of one wall.

## Multiplayer and persistence
- **The server checks every control** (`tv/JugcraftTv.control`). It must be the screen's bottom-left block. The player must be within 56 blocks of the screen's nearest block (48, and 8 for walking about with the window open), and in the owner's party (or creative). A window asked for from afar (`JugcraftTv.ask`) must be for a TV block within 50 blocks. A link must be an http(s) address with a host and no spaces or control characters, at most 1024 characters.
- **Clocks:** each block's update packet carries the server's clock ("now"), and the game receiving it keeps the difference, so players' own clocks don't matter.
- **Joining and splitting** (`tv/FlatTvBlock.rejoin`) runs:
  - when a TV block is placed;
  - when one is removed beside others;
  - a tick after the block behind a TV changes.
  It sets each block's edges (LEFT, RIGHT, UP, DOWN as you look at it) and STAND, and moves what the screens played to the new ones: the biggest screen with a link wins. Breaking the bottom-left block hands its link to the block beside it first.
- **Saved** on the bottom-left block: owner, link, start time, paused and where, volume, replay and whole playlist. Every block keeps its placer.
- **Older worlds:** a 3 x 2 "flat_tv" from before loads as six single screens. Right-click it, or place or break a block beside it, and it joins up.

## Dependencies and assets
- **The video decoder:** FFmpeg 8.1.2 (LGPL-2.1+ build by Bytedeco, `org.bytedeco:ffmpeg:8.1.2-1.5.14`) through JavaCV and JavaCPP 1.5.14 (Apache-2.0 / GPLv2 with Classpath Exception). It is not inside Jugcraft's jar (docs/FRAMEWORKS.md). Instead:
  - The first time a game plays a TV, `client/tv/TvCodec.java` downloads five jars from Maven Central (repo1.maven.org) into `.minecraft/jugcraft/tv-helpers/lib`: JavaCV, JavaCPP, FFmpeg, and the JavaCPP and FFmpeg natives for that computer only (Windows x64, Linux x64 or arm64, macOS). That is about 35 MB on Windows.
  - Each jar is checked against the SHA-1 Maven Central publishes for it.
  - They are loaded in a class loader of their own with `client/tv/TvFfmpeg.java`. The rest of the mod reaches them only through `client/tv/TvMedia.java`.
  - The build has them compile-only.
  - FFmpeg may only use http, https, tls, tcp and crypto (`protocol_whitelist`), so a playlist can't point it at a local file. Links to the player's own computer or local network aren't played.
- **The YouTube helper:** YouTube pages aren't videos. `client/tv/TvHelpers.java` asks **yt-dlp** (github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp, Unlicense) for the page's streams, of YouTube's own clients and the Safari web client (which has the HLS streams): HLS H.264 at most 720p first, then DASH H.264 at most 720p with the best sound (else anything but AV1), then anything. yt-dlp needs a JavaScript engine for YouTube, **Deno** (github.com/denoland/deno, MIT), unless one is installed already.
  - Neither ships with the mod. The first YouTube link a player plays, or the window's **GET HELPER** button, downloads both from their latest GitHub releases into `.minecraft/jugcraft/tv-helpers`. On Windows that is about 22 MB for yt-dlp and 42 MB for Deno.
  - Each is checked against the SHA-256 digest GitHub publishes for it (or the release's checksum file).
  - A player who only watches is asked first: their screen says YOUTUBE HELPER NEEDED and the window has the button.
  - When yt-dlp stops working with YouTube, the newest is fetched, at most twice a day.
- **Assets:**
  - Models: `tools/flat_tv.py`, 32 models for 128 states, called by `tools/plant_interior.py`.
  - Textures: tv_body, tv_glass, tv_back, tv_metal, tv_led.
  - The flat icon is the 16 x 16 one `tools/nuclear_item_icons.py` draws (docs/ITEM_ICONS.md).
  - The renderer is `client/tv/TvRenderer.java`.
  - The speaker: `tools/tv_speaker.py` writes its meshes (`tower_mesh/tv_speaker.bin.gz`, `tv_speaker_cone.bin.gz`: the surround, cone and dust cap the renderer moves), their atlas `textures/entity/tv_speaker.png`, its empty block model, both 16 x 16 item icons (drawn pixel by pixel), the loot table, recipes and names; `tools/tv_speaker_data.py` holds the names and the loot table for the material data generator too (which lists the speaker as a machine block and the key as a machine item). Small parts stand at least 6 mm off what they're on; the basket's wall runs on behind the surround so the moving cone never opens a gap.
  - `tools/tv_speaker.py` builds its meshes with the Drone Tower's mesh tools (`tools/drone_parts_shelving.py`, `tools/parts_shelving_parts.py`), which aren't on main yet: what it wrote is committed, and it runs again once they are.
  - Sizes: 1 px = 6.25 cm. Bezel 0.25 px at the sides and top, chin 0.55 px, lip 0.12 px proud of the glass. The picture is 0.09 px in front of the glass, and the logo and light 0.1 px in front of the lip: no faces fight.

## Rollout and open questions
- **Privacy:** a screen's link makes the game of every player near it fetch that address. The owner's party chooses links. Local-network addresses are refused, and only the web protocols are allowed.
- Video and sound play only within 96 blocks (the renderer's reach), and at most four screens at once per game (the nearest).
- Live streams play from their live edge, with no seeking.
- A link whose host YouTube changes, or that a site blocks, shows CAN'T PLAY THIS with the reason.
