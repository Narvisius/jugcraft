# Industrial production lines and factory layouts

Status: proposed connected production and layout plan, 10 October 2026. The 36 route cards explain useful inputs, shared stages, products, entry and accounting. Eight block-grid layouts give practical expansion examples. Existing selected directions remain authoritative; these layouts and remaining operating defaults are proposals for review.

## Reading the production cards

A route names a useful specialty rather than a required quest. Material can be purchased or traded from another player, and earlier compatible recipes stay available until replacements are reachable. Shared bodies can run different compatible recipes or installed tools; each change still requires the right capability.

Fixed water/gas figures below come from the existing starter planning baseline. No chemical preparation temperatures, real weapon formulations or uncontrolled process recipes are added. Mineral, solution, fuel and precursor quantities without a complete ledger remain implementation data to specify before gameplay, not certified yields.

## Workshop foundations

### Ceramic tooling and workshop alloy stock

**Route:** Clay and earlier mineral supplies → shaping and firing → insulators, crucibles, liners and reusable molds → compatible workshop alloying.

The kiln serves construction ceramics as well as useful decorative brick families. Ordinary ceramic tooling gives the crucible a reachable entry; suitable heat upgrades later add the chosen quartz/charcoal silicon capability. Bronze/brass casting can use a reusable gear mold at the same metal cost as the plate recipe.

Keep material stock distinct from fuel. Four compatible ingots cast one gear only where that same existing gear costs four plates; a fired mold is not consumed or refunded every batch. Different heat capabilities do not grant advanced alloy recipes automatically.

### Mechanical components and first electricity

**Route:** Earlier metal stock → hammer plates and wire cutters → shared shaft-driven forming/assembly → dynamo or small fuel generator → useful powered consumer.

The first press, drawer and assembler can be constructed using hand-made parts and a reachable soldering tool. Motors later drive the same workshop shafts. A short working electrical line is a meaningful milestone; large batteries, aluminum cables and completion of the textiles industry are optional.

Manual and compatible machine routes retain equal material yield. Basic controls have a manual producer before the assembler exists. Work/time costs belong to operation, while reusable soldering tools and dies belong to installed capability.

### Steel and bulk casting

**Route:** Iron plus reachable charcoal → initial Steel Foundry; coal → Coke Oven → economical bulk steel → larger press and optional casting bay.

Build the first ordinary steel without requiring coke. Coke improves sustained production, and larger ovens can support a larger foundry. Reusable dies supply plates, rods and structural shapes. The casting bay is a paid useful addition for compatible stock and molds, with its own location inside the 5×6×6 foundry envelope.

One iron ingot contributes nine metal units to one steel ingot. Carbon pays for processing rather than extra metal. Compare oven supply and foundry demand; a faster foundry cannot consume free coke or bypass its heat capability.

## Agricultural workshops and finished goods

### Seed oils and coatings

**Route:** Suitable oilseed crops → preparation → Screw Press → named oil and press cake → kettle/finishing recipes → coating, binder and useful farm products.

Flax keeps its drying-oil specialty, while other oils can satisfy explicitly compatible uses. The press also supports fruit juice with a separate head/receipt. A modest workshop can sell oil and cake immediately, then invest in coatings, linoleum binder or later reactor fuel chemistry.

Allocate oil and cake from the same harvest once. Only approved cake types enter animal feed. Later biodiesel consumes the defined oil/alcohol/reagent receipt and assigns glycerin separately; natural oil is not relabeled directly into diesel.

### Prepared fibre yarn and cloth

**Route:** Flax/cotton/wool preparation → crop/card preparation → spinning → powered or hand weaving → cloth, canvas, clothing stock and reinforced goods.

Use recipe-specific preparation heads rather than giving every crop a universal fibre output. Spinning preserves existing hand-wheel behavior. Loom fixtures select useful weaves or patterns; reinforcement adds its own paid stock. Electricity improves handling through motor drive without demanding a new loom for ordinary cloth.

Each harvest receipt allocates fibres, seeds and residue once. Powered weaving improves work rate, not fibre creation. Cloth patterns are sidegrades unless a product's actual function requires reinforcement.

### Cordage nets and fibre mats

**Route:** Prepared yarn/fibre → Rope Maker or carding/felting → cordage, nets, pads, insulation and appropriate filter products.

The rope station's long bed gives several strands a readable shared path. Carding/felting uses a distinct drum-and-mat mechanism. Builders, fishers, farmers and workshop users receive useful alternatives without needing the chemical tier. Suitable reinforced belts keep existing leather-belt routes valid.

Declare strand count and output length consistently; cutting or unmaking rope cannot return more fibre than was used. Filters and felting tooling are reusable equipment capabilities unless a particular finished consumable is explicitly selected.

### Paper sheets and packaging

**Route:** Suitable wood/stem stock → Mill and Pulper → compatible wet preparation → Sheet Former/press → drying → paper and useful packaging.

Start with simple vat/screen preparation and later use the long sheet former. Shared finishing handles appropriate coloration or surface treatment. Packaging earns its recipe role through bulk handling, provisions or a real consumer; ordinary books/maps and eating remain reachable.

Account for pulp, water, trim and finished sheets. Recovered water is a paid process result, not a second whole batch of pulp. Clean trim can feed a lossful compatible recovery receipt; it does not duplicate finished paper.

### Boards veneers and laminates

**Route:** Sawmill stock or prepared fibre → adhesive preparation → mat/veneer arrangement → Heated Panel Press → drying/finish → panels, crates, furniture and selected housings.

Give the basic board route a reachable binder and modest heating. Stronger laminates can use better adhesive and controlled pressing. This line connects foresters, farmers, builders and machinery suppliers while keeping refractory ceramics and other structural materials useful.

Wood/fibre, adhesive and decorative veneer are separate inputs. A decorative face does not grant universal blast resistance or insulation. Any benefit attaches to an explicit block, component or compatible structure.

### Linoleum flooring and surface finishes

**Route:** Linseed oil/resin binder plus wood flour/mineral filler/pigment → kettle → backed sheet rolling → curing → floor coverings, tiles and matching panels.

One entry binder step and one forming/curing receipt support a useful product line. Patterned rollers or stock arrangements produce solid, marbled, striped and checker forms. The long roller and dryer are shared with other workshops, so a flooring producer need not buy a machine for each pattern.

Backing cloth, binder and filler are paid once. Pattern variants have explicit equivalent surface yield. Curing pays work and retains/removes declared material; raw pressed residue is not automatically a finished construction filler.

### Natural rubber and compatible flexible goods

**Route:** Tree latex → earlier vinegar-compatible preparation → rolling/drying → paid sulfur/heat curing → sheets, grips, gaskets and compatible reinforced goods.

Basic optional rubber uses a reachable workshop bench and heat, not the first industrial acid plant. Existing synthetic rubber later supplies compatible ordinary products. A reinforced belt or specialist gasket needs an actual reinforcement/compound receipt; synthetic stock is not universally superior.

Raw latex, carrier loss and dried stock have fixed receipts. Molds remain reusable. PTFE chemical-resistant parts have a separate route; ordinary rubber never satisfies every demanding process simply because it seals a pipe.

### Feed bedding and formed fuel supplies

**Route:** Eligible grain/seed/residue → milling/mixing → forming/drying where useful → animal-specific feed, bedding, compost blends or briquettes.

Use purposeful ingredient tags for different livestock/crop consumers. Provide bulk provisions through existing cooking and approved drying rather than requiring excessive animal entities. Forming has a reason when it improves handling or a fuel/product application; ordinary feeding and composting stay accessible.

Allocate edible feed and fuel from the same input once. Fertilizer/crop/seed loops need bounded yield. A briquette's burn value cannot exceed its eligible ingredient fuel budget just because it was pressed into a new shape.

## Starter gas and acid supplies

### Water and ordinary brine separation

**Route:** Water → Separator → H2/O2; ordinary salt brine → Separator → H2/chlorine/lye → generators and useful chemical feeds.

Use the selected steel/basic-control entry hardware and three fixed output roles. Lye retains and blocks production when full; water recipes leave the lye destination unused. The player sees the specific full output, rather than losing chlorine/lye to an implicit vent.

Retain the water anchor of 1,000 liquid mB → 500 gas mB H2 + 250 gas mB O2. The starter brine draft uses 400 ticks at 256 JE/t. Electrolysis efficiency limits still apply to every enlarged form and hydrogen-consuming generator.

### Hydrogen chloride and aqueous acid

**Route:** H2 plus chlorine → suitable Infuser → HCl gas → shared absorption with water → ordinary aqueous HCl and named consumers.

A dedicated Infuser can make HCl while another performs methane or sulfur conversion. The wet reactor handles absorption; the Rotary Condensator does not impersonate dissolution. HCl supplies compatible magnesium preparation and later selected food/polymer uses, preserving separate gas and solution handling.

The retained draft is 250 gas mB H2 + 250 chlorine → 500 HCl gas, then the separately defined aqueous receipt. Solution composition must be formalized before concentration/recovery recipes; no free gas-to-liquid relabeling.

### Sulfuric backbone and acid grades

**Route:** Existing sulfur/O2 → Oxidizer → SO2 → contact-bed Infuser → SO3 → paid-carrier absorption → ordinary acid → concentrated or purified demanding supply.

The first contact bed has an independent earlier mineral route and the absorber keeps a paid starter carrier. Ordinary acid supports useful initial chemistry. Concentrated supply and electronic purity are distinct transformations; the first purifier uses earlier compatible controls and construction.

Use the existing starter SO2/SO3/ordinary-acid baseline. Concentration must conserve acid and remove declared water; purification removes defined impurities/losses. Numerical solution receipts remain unavailable until the composition ledger is complete, rather than guessed from equal fluid volume.

### Coal derived syngas and methane

**Route:** Coal/coke or lower-yield charcoal plus water and paid heat → Gasifier → composition-defined dirty mix → combined cleanup/separation → CO/H2 → nickel methanation or useful H2.

The first gasifier needs no separate oxygen feed; oxygen assistance is a later paid improvement. Combined cleanup supplies usable gas and one mixed residue. A dedicated methane Infuser uses the permanent nickel bed and a separate water destination. Coke can earn its bulk role through existing source economy and compatible processing.

Define carbon reference amounts for each feed and all residue before fixing gasifier yields. Separation splits a declared mixture once. Methanation retains 100 CO + 300 H2 → 100 CH4 + 200 liquid water, with the selected drain-on-full water default.

### Captured carbon dioxide and upgraded methane

**Route:** Optional brewery/cement/other accounted CO2 capture plus H2 → nickel Infuser → methane and recovered water → shared gas-burning generation.

Capture can be added when compact fuel is useful. Full optional CO2 storage vents by default unless the player chooses stop-instead. Pure methane has better energy density per equal-size tank and higher selected generator output; a captured waste stream does not make the hydrogen electricity free.

Retain 100 CO2 + 400 H2 → 100 CH4 + 400 liquid water. At the draft 256 JE/mB H2 minimum, H2 alone costs 102,400 JE per batch; the draft maximum total methane recovery is 67,200 JE before synthesis cost. The loop remains lossy.

## Mineral refining and useful alloys

### Regional mineral concentration

**Route:** Selected mineral-bearing sand/rock → screening and washing → gravity/magnetic separation → suitable later electrostatic separation → named concentrate fractions.

Ordinary beach sand is not the selected source of every mineral. Regional deposits make bulk feed discoverable without incentivizing stripping every beach. One preparation plant can sell concentrates to several specialists; late separation adds meaningful product grades rather than a compulsory refinement step for unrelated common stone.

Each registered feed has declared mineral fractions, rejects and water costs. Concentrates are not generic interchangeable slurry. A named vanadium fraction requires a specifically vanadium-bearing feed, while cobalt's selected primary supply is a separate ore.

### Coastal magnesium supply

**Route:** Pump in eligible beach/ocean biome → magnesium-bearing coastal feed → concentration → hydroxide/chloride preparation → dry chloride feed → compatible molten electrolysis → magnesium/chlorine.

Use the shared concentrator, compatible wet vessels, filtration and thermal preparation rather than a machine for each salt. First hardware uses earlier copper conductors and suitable ceramics. Titanium salt return supplements this line later; it cannot supply the first magnesium.

Coastal magnesium feed differs from ordinary sodium salt brine and inland fresh water. Hydroxide/HCl/dehydration receipts account for added reagents and removed water. Molten conversion preserves magnesium/chlorine units and pays work; sodium brine does not secretly become magnesium.

### Alumina aluminum and lye recovery

**Route:** Prepared bauxite → appropriate alkaline digestion → filtration/precipitation → calcining → suitable electrolysis with carbon feed → aluminum → forming and useful products.

Aluminum supplies conductors, lighter panels, frames and selected vehicle/process parts. The first cell cannot require the aluminum cable it is meant to produce. Share wet processing and the calciner; alkaline recovery reduces demand through explicit paid receipts rather than making the whole line reagent-free.

Account for aluminum content, carbon consumption, outgoing gas, lye return/loss and distinct bauxite residue. Stabilized filler consumes prepared residue and a paid binder. Do not award extra aluminum from a residue already assigned to the main product.

### Titanium preparation reduction and cleanup

**Route:** Named titanium mineral concentrate → compatible treatment/chloride preparation → distillation/purification → magnesium reduction → integrated sponge cleanup/salt return → compatible melting/forming.

The retort includes first-installation cleanup and salt handling, with dedicated clean-product and return outlets. Titanium's chosen products include stronger suitable equipment above steel and useful precision/vehicle applications. Shared distillation handles this chemistry separately from ordinary oil fractionation recipes.

Magnesium consumption and magnesium-chloride return are paired in the ledger. Electrolysis can recover some later magnesium with paid energy; neither salt recycling nor reusable retort tooling creates free metal. Removed impurities and incomplete recovery remain accounted.

### Stainless and aluminum magnesium alloy products

**Route:** Chromite concentrate → shared chromium preparation; earlier nickel/iron/steel plus chromium → stainless stock; independently supplied aluminum plus magnesium → structural alloy stock.

Use compatible upgraded alloying and the shared press/casting families. Stainless enables named process vessels/fittings; aluminum-magnesium supplies lighter frames and panels. Builders can buy the alloy stock without operating every refinery. Exact commercial grades are not a new registry ladder.

Start with whole-ingot game blend batches whose total metal units equal the output stock. Chromium/nickel/magnesium contributions are actually consumed. A lighter frame is not automatically better in every heat, reagent or armor application.

### Rare earth fractions and advanced consumers

**Route:** Suitable monazite/other named concentrate → compatible dissolution → filtering and chemical separation → named fractions/stock → magnets, precision parts and other defined consumers.

Keep this marked follow-up broad enough to expand without forcing the full rare-earth industry into first chemistry. The large Chemical Separation Rack has several process stages visually, but recipes can run through useful shared modules. Existing earlier magnets remain reachable for the first separation hardware.

Only declared feed fractions produce the requested named elements. Separation is paid, and each rejected/recovered fraction is assigned once. Avoid one dust that becomes every rare-earth stock at full yield or a separator requiring its own first output.

## Industrial reagents polymers and fuels

### Phosphate fertilizers and prepared gypsum products

**Route:** Phosphate rock → wet digestion → filtration/recovery → phosphoric supply → triple-superphosphate or other compatible products; suitable gypsum-rich residue → paid preparation → construction ingredients.

A shared forming/mixing line makes named fertilizer stock and optional mixed-farm blends. Nitrogen/phosphate applications refer to broad crop groups, with ordinary compost still useful. The residue branch gives builders a real outlet only after its preparation receipt earns the finished ingredient.

Fertilizer consumes nutrient stock and is applied once. Blends cannot stack the full benefit of all ingredients for free. Raw residue is not placed as finished plaster/board; purification, removed fractions, binder, energy and losses are specified separately.

### Fluorite hydrofluoric acid and glass work

**Route:** Selected snow-biome fluorite → preparation → compatible heated concentrated-acid processing → HF stream/calcium-sulfate output → compatible solution/recovery → oxide/glass and specialty-material consumers.

Underground deposits with occasional outcrops support discovery and trade. The first compatible chamber is reachable before HF/PTFE exists. Advanced chips use HF for its selected oxide job, while suitable etched/frosted glass can be a decorative sidegrade.

Use the chemical catalog's balanced fluorite relationship and its separate gas/aqueous representation. Calcium-sulfate output is not duplicated into both a free construction product and a full residue recovery credit. HF is not a universal metal/silicon etchant.

### Ordinary plastics and forming

**Route:** Refinery or later paid ethanol-derived ethylene → suitable HDPE/LDPE polymerization; named chloride intermediates → PVC → stock → molding/extrusion → useful finished parts.

Rigid housings/panels, appropriate insulation, hoses, gaskets and vehicle panels have actual consumers. Molding produces discrete shapes with reusable molds; extrusion produces continuous stock with a compatible die. Ordinary PE/PVC substitutions use explicit tags while specialist parts retain their material requirements.

Different grades are defined recipes/capabilities, not a player pipe-pressure dial. Pellets, sheet stock, trim and finished goods share one mass receipt. Clean offcuts can recover the same grade with power and losses; mixed waste cannot become PTFE.

### PTFE and chemical equipment construction

**Route:** HF plus independently supplied compatible carbon/intermediate feeds → named fluorochemical precursor/monomer stages → polymerization → forming → PTFE seals, fittings and liners.

This is a short game-material branch with explicit intermediate identities and compatible stations, not HF renamed into plastic. The first precursor/polymer line uses earlier compatible construction. Finished PTFE parts then enable selected demanding later machinery without recurring lining replacement.

Exact fluorochemical input/output receipts remain a ledger task before implementation. Preserve fluorine/carbon and account for side streams in each stage. A sealed vessel only gains the specifically supported capability; PTFE parts are consumed in construction once.

### Bioethanol and fermentation capture

**Route:** Eligible crop stock → milling and mashing → fermentation → distillation → Bio Generator; later demanding uses add dehydration and fuel/blend compatibility.

Early barrel/fuel-still routes remain useful; steel controlled equipment adds handling and capture. Fermentation can supply concentrated CO2 to a storage/capture branch. The Bio Generator accepts suitable bioethanol with the proposed 96 JE/t entry rate; fuel energy and burn duration still need the shared receipt.

Cropping and processing allocate sugar/starch, residue, alcohol, water and CO2 once. Drying/dehydration is a separate paid capability for demanding consumers. Ethanol-to-ethylene is a later useful alternative with conversion losses, not a free fuel/monomer switch.

### Anaerobic digestion and biogas upgrading

**Route:** Eligible organic waste plus declared wet feed → 6×6×6 digester → composition-defined raw biogas/digestate → cleanup → Bio Generator or separation/methanation → pure methane.

The large digester is a substantial installation, not required before first H2, acid or ethanol. Define eligible food/farm residues and digestate uses to avoid a universal waste-to-everything recipe. Cleaned mixed biogas powers the lower-output Bio Generator; pure methane powers the shared gas generator.

For the selected 60/40 mixture, 1,000 reference gas mB split into 600 CH4 and 400 CO2. Converting the CO2 needs 1,600 H2 and produces another 400 CH4 plus 1,600 liquid water: final methane is 1,000, paid H2 is additional. No pure methane and full raw mixture are awarded together.

### Synthetic crude and shared refined fuels

**Route:** Accounted coal/biomass/gas conversion → cleaned conditioned CO/H2 supply → cobalt/ceramic synthesis → one synthetic-crude mixture plus water → shared distillation/upgrading → compatible finished fuels.

Primary cobalt ore has an independent preparation route; its permanent bed differs from nickel methanation tooling. The 6×6×6 train combines a recognizable synthesis vessel, condensers and water/output receivers. Existing refinery columns/cracking capabilities produce finished gasoline and other fractions.

Synthetic origin does not increase the value of an identical finished fuel. Allocate crude fractions once and include feedstock, H2, heating, cleanup and all refining costs. A gas mixture, crude stock and finished gasoline cannot all retain the full feed energy after the feed is spent.

## Precision electronics and control products

### High purity wafers and general advanced chips

**Route:** Separately prepared high-purity silicon → compatible shared crystal/wafer production → precision cutting/polishing → one photoresist coat → pattern exposure → development/appropriate etching/cleaning → chip assembly.

Reuse the existing Arc Furnace crystal capability and saw/assembly families where compatible; do not recreate the removed standalone Crystal Grower. Novolac resin plus a DNQ additive supplies one initial resist. The wet station and lithography station remain separate, with ground-level interfaces and independently reachable first controls.

Cleaning, development and etching have distinct reagent/tool requirements. TMAH is the developer and HF is the selected oxide treatment; no universal etchant substitutes for all layers. Consume stock/reagents and assign one accounted spent mixture. Reusable pattern plates do not wear or become finished chips.

### Speed efficiency and local automation modules

**Route:** One general advanced chip plus function-related earlier parts → shared Module Assembler → speed, efficiency or local automation module → compatible existing/large machinery.

Speed uses suitable drive/control parts, efficiency uses power-control parts, and automation uses its declared control/memory/interface stock. Exact component names resolve against shared identities before registration. Recipe information displays real supported effects; a small station can benefit without purchasing a bulk plant.

The chip is consumed once in one module. Existing module IDs/calculations need a compatibility mapping. A speed module increases working draw rather than creating free energy, efficiency stops at recipe limits, and local stock targets never authorize remote world scans.

### Later silane film deposition

**Route:** Independently prepared compatible silane feed plus existing substrate → sealed deposition chamber → defined silicon film substrate → later compatible chip/process stages.

This adds one distinctive advanced process after the first wafer line works. The chamber has a long clean enclosure, service head and protected gas delivery. A deposited film has an actual compatible consumer; it does not replace all bulk silicon, wafer preparation or lithography.

Silane preparation requires a separate paid precursor receipt before implementation. Deposition preserves substrate identity and accounts for gas products and silicon content. The new capability does not award both a coated substrate and all unspent starting gas.

## General storage cryogenics and expedition supplies

### Lead acid and lithium ion storage

**Route:** Galena/earlier lead supply plus ordinary sulfuric electrolyte → lead-acid bank; later spodumene preparation → lithium stock → LiPF6 salt → independent solvent formulation → electrodes/separators/cell assembly → lithium bank.

Lead-acid can accompany steel chemistry and buffer modest workshops. Lithium improves a later general storage option; portable/specialty packs remain deferred. Formulation uses one defined compatible solvent stock with an independent earlier producer rather than an undefined universal liquid.

Salt preparation and formulated electrolyte are two distinct paid stages. Electrode/separator parts are also consumed; electrolyte does not grant free initial electrical charge. Preserve old lithium/lead identities and consumers during transition. Exact solution receipts/capacities remain balance data to complete.

### Vanadium electrolyte and modular flow banks

**Route:** Named vanadium mineral supply or preserved refinery-residue route → appropriate refining/formulation → paired compatible electrolyte sides → controller/cell stack/attached capacity pair → charge and discharge.

The 6×6×6 reference bank is a major grid-storage purchase. A complete tank pair controls capacity; a compatible cell-stack module controls charging/discharge output. The proposed first arrangement has one paired bay, with another complete bank expanding the grid within the machine size rule.

Capacity is based on the limiting valid paired supply, not twice a single unpaired tank. All charge lives in one controller ledger. No module grants energy by placement or reattachment, and no routine cycle aging is introduced. Detachment follows the explicit remaining-capacity rule.

### Progressive cryogenic rocket supplies

**Route:** Existing oxygen cooling → compatible insulated storage; later upgraded methane cooling → methane/LOX roles; more advanced hydrogen cooling → LH2/LOX roles.

The ordinary Rotary Condensator retains suitable phase-conversion jobs. Rocket liquids use the upgraded Cryogenic Liquefier, with specific capability and separate tanks. Existing RP-1/LOX and terrestrial rocket consumers remain reachable while new engine/fuel compatibility is designed. Hydrazine precedes MMH in the separately selected game station/maneuvering branch.

Declare each gas/liquid reference conversion independently; the water anchor does not determine cryogenic density. Liquefaction and reverse conversion conserve chemical amounts and pay work. No automatic boil-off or engine-damage upkeep is invented, and no real propellant mixture recipe is specified here.

## Recovery and readable factory consequences

### Equipment scrap and spent stream recovery

**Route:** Defined retired equipment → disassembly → sorted/prepared scrap → compatible remelting; eligible spent streams → filtration/neutralization/distillation → accounted reuse and residual emission.

Manual disassembly is an early useful service. Scrap preparation feeds the same alloy/foundry families; chemical treatment uses shared wet stations. The Particle Collector and compatible gas-cleaning rack reduce the appropriate outgoing contribution. The map device shows pollution and automatic marauder eligibility without changing the world appearance.

A dismantled item yields its recipe-specific reusable parts plus losses, not a full build refund and another copy of installed tools. Treatment consumes the spent stream once. Emissions follow actual processing/fuel use; existing warnings, cooldown, active-raid guards and strength caps remain.

## Block grid layout conventions

Building sizes are **width × depth × clear height**, distinct from each machine's **width × depth × height**. Every machine below uses 2–6 blocks per axis; the room itself can be larger. Coordinates are zero-based local floor coordinates **x, z** from the northwest corner. All listed bodies occupy half-open rectangles [x, x+width) and [z, z+depth); their operating front faces smaller z. No rotation is assumed in these examples.

Allow at least two clear blocks on main operating aisles and two clear blocks above the tallest listed machine. Side service gaps and pipe corridors are part of the building layout, not hidden machine size. Port coordinates still need actual model/form diagrams; these room layouts do not invent a socket on every face.

A named reactor/Infuser in two places is two independently paid stations. Layouts show separate useful lines sharing capabilities, not a request to construct all machinery before any production. Existing portable tanks, dynamos, cables and simple transfer hardware retain their own current sizes; a service pad does not inflate them into a new two-block machine.

## Recipe ledger before implementation

Give every registered feed a bounded composition/grade entry. Dirty gas and biogas use fixed declared mixtures, not arbitrary per-molecule simulation. A separator's summed products fit that feed. Solution conversion tracks active material, carrier water and impurities; it cannot deduce acid purity from visual color or create a new grade at equal volume without a receipt.

For metal/alloy routes, declare retained element fractions, slag/reject losses and carbon/reagent costs. For crop routes, assign harvest mass once to food/fibre/seed/cake/residue. For polymer offcuts, preserve grade and limit recovery. For salt returns, distinguish reusable material from the work needed to restore useful metal. For batteries, distinguish contained electrolyte/capacity from paid electrical charge.

Default output policies and state transitions come from the construction/operation plan. Missing composition or unsupported phase capability blocks the proposed recipe's implementation until its data is complete. That does not make an unrelated already working route unusable.

The eight proposed layouts contain **71 machine placements**. This geometric check does not certify exact socket access, collision/rendering, multiplayer, transfer throughput or in-game performance. The production cards preserve reusable shared stations, early entry, useful sidegrades and the selected gas/acid/metal delivery direction.
