# Hearth Oven and canning automation analysis

Read-only source analysis on 7 October 2026, OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), on `peepo-companion` after Cider Press logistics. This records the original recommendations. [Canning Kettle automation](companion-canning.md) has since been implemented, including planner assignment, water setup, jar transport and spoilage recovery. [Hearth Oven automation](companion-hearth.md) is now implemented as well; the original recommendations below remain background analysis.

## Hearth Oven: one pie, fuel and a collection deadline

Sources: [HearthOvenBlockEntity](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/agriculture/HearthOvenBlockEntity.java), [HearthOvenBlock](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/agriculture/HearthOvenBlock.java), [PieFilling](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/agriculture/PieFilling.java), [GeneratorFuels](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/machine/GeneratorFuels.java).

- Right-click a raw pie to insert it into an empty oven; right-click fuel to add its burn time. Raw apple, pumpkin cream, cranberry, sweet potato and chestnut pies are supported. They are crafted upstream, not assembled from ingredients inside the oven. For example, raw apple pie is pastry dough, two apples and sugar in shapeless crafting.
- One pie fits at a time. Fuel banks up to 3,200 ticks: tagged hearth wood supplies 300 ticks, charcoal 1,200, coal 1,600 and coke 3,200. Coal blocks return a nominal 16,000 ticks from the shared fuel helper but cannot fit this oven's bank, so a porter must exclude them even though `isFuel()` returns true.
- The oven heats one degree per two ticks while burning, up to 100, and cools one per four ticks without fuel. Pies advance only at heat 50 or above: one baking point per tick, or two at 100. Baked is 600 points; burnt is 1,200. At constant maximum heat that is 15 seconds until baked and another 15 seconds until burnt, at 20 TPS. Cold starts, cooling and low TPS change elapsed wall time.
- Empty-hand right-click removes the pie immediately, even if raw or burnt. The comparator reports 0 empty, 1-13 baking, 15 baked, 1 burnt. Breaking the oven drops the pie in its current state.
- Public hopper/fluid connections remain absent. The new companion ports use transactions instead of the player-only manual feed/take methods; no fabricated player is used.

Implemented direction (see the Hearth Oven record for actual behavior): select the desired finished pie in the companion GUI, fetch its matching raw pie and suitable fuel from Supply, tend the oven, then move the cooked pie into physical cargo promptly before delivering it to Output. Extract only baked or burnt pies; never accidentally take a raw pie during normal output probing. A ready pie needs urgent collection ahead of new supply runs. Ensure cargo/output space before loading, and keep the worker near an active oven rather than making a long supply trip. If production should remain safe while a worker eats, rests or loses access, add an explicit local collection tray and define when a physically present helper moves the pie into it; do not silently disable burning or remotely rescue a pie. Fuel, heat and burn timing should remain server-owned. Add transactional input/output methods, persistence and normal access checks before registering the job.

## Canning: Cooking Pot first, Kettle second

Sources: [CanningKettleBlockEntity](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/agriculture/CanningKettleBlockEntity.java), [CanningKettleBlock](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/agriculture/CanningKettleBlock.java), [PreserveJarItem](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/agriculture/PreserveJarItem.java), [CookingPotBlockEntity](../../src/main/java/io/github/jimbozoomer/jugcraft/agriculture/CookingPotBlockEntity.java).

- The Cooking Pot creates filled, unsealed preserve jars. Its existing ghost recipe and companion transport already cover the recipe's ingredients and Mason Jar. Apple Butter, for example, needs one Mason Jar, three apples, sugar and Sweet Cider. The Canning Kettle seals an already filled jar; it does not fill empty jars or cook raw ingredients.
- Right-click the kettle with one water bucket. Water remains between batches and is not consumed by sealing. An empty bucket removes it only while the kettle contains no jars. The kettle needs a block in `jugcraft:heat_sources` directly below it, lit if it has a lit property.
- Up to four full, unsealed, unspoiled jars fit at once. Starting cold, heat reaches boiling after 200 ticks (10 seconds at 20 TPS); each jar then requires 400 boiling ticks (20 seconds). Jars process concurrently. Removing heat cools the water and pauses sealing without discarding accumulated processing time.
- Unsealed preserves spoil after more than 72,000 game ticks from their cooked timestamp (three Minecraft days). Partly eaten or already spoiled jars cannot be sealed. A jar that spoils inside the kettle remains unsealed and needs removal, so an automation adapter needs a reject/recovery path rather than waiting forever.
- Sealed jars do not spoil until opened; eating starts their new shelf life. Empty-hand right-click lifts sealed jars; shift-empty-hand lifts all jars. The comparator measures sealed count. Breaking the kettle drops the actual jar stacks, with their components intact.
- The kettle has no public item/fluid transfer registration. Its new companion-only ports preserve jar components and progress transactionally; sealed jars, unchanged spoiled rejects and the returned empty water bucket go to Output. See the implementation record linked above.

Implemented adapter: fetch a water bucket once if needed and return the empty bucket, load fresh filled jars from Supply, and carry sealed jars to Output. Heating and sealing already run independently; the first version needs no speed bonus or continuous helper. Use the general interaction clip at loading/unloading if desired. Output-full simply leaves sealed jars safely in the rack. A Cooking Pot's Output chest can be a different companion's kettle Supply chest; the current per-companion assignment rules prohibit giving one chest both roles to the same companion. The base kettle does not require recipe selection because it processes any eligible preserve jar; an optional filter could limit varieties later.

## Suggested order and server cost

Kettle logistics is now implemented first: it reuses physical transport, has four bounded item positions and no collection deadline for finished products. Hearth Oven tending now follows it, including collection priority and interruption handling for burning. Both should add explicit adapters to the shared job/transport contracts rather than independent world searches. Reuse loaded-only assignments, owner/town permissions, scheduled searches, bounded storage views, cargo transactions and backoff. No chunk tickets, fake players, global workstation scans or additional machine-wide polling are needed.

No gameplay or automated tests were run for this analysis. Existing behavior is derived from source, not observed in a running world.
