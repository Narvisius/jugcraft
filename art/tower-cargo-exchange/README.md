# Original closed Cargo Exchange artwork

Created for Jugcraft by OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), 8 October 2026, under the repository's MIT license.

The owner's corrected design is a completely closed-in logistics box in the tower's black, charcoal and gray palette. `tools/tower_cargo_exchange.py` authors the continuous opaque steel housing, gray mineral plinth, gasketed removable covers, roof hatches, hinges, captive fasteners, recessed pulls, backed ventilation and clipped service conduit. The operator readout and item union are integrated into the front bulkhead. There are no exposed rollers, conveyor, open inspection tunnel, products or additional input opening. The power reference establishes the amount of detail, not its cabinet design or paint colors.

All geometry, neutral finishes, mineral-base texture and markings are original deterministic code-native assets. No power-cabinet, reference-image, tower-texture, third-party or owner-library pixels are copied. The repository art direction, license policy and owner-library catalog were reviewed. The 29 source PNGs in `originals/` are copied byte-for-byte to their `tower_cargo_*` runtime paths; `provenance.json` records their dimensions and SHA-256 values. Previously authored texture filenames remain stable, including unused filenames associated with the superseded open design.

Run `python tools/tower_cargo_exchange.py --write --check --preview` for the cargo-only writer and actual exported-model preview. The shared `tools/tower_exchanges.py` calls the same geometry, texture and item functions. All 60 active native parts retain the original 5 by 4 by 3 footprint, part order, anchor and port 22. Unused states 60 through 63 and their empty assets remain unchanged. The world assembly has 274 authored closed solids and 2,592 exported faces; the inventory model preserves the complete closed housing and major attached components at its smaller scale.

`tower_cargo_exchange_panels.json` records three coarse collision volumes for the foundation, closed housing and integrated bulkhead. It retains the existing blank display and pilot-lens planes. Gameplay supplies the actual stock, occupied-slot and request figures; artwork contains no fake live values or motion. The sole functional connection remains the six-pixel square item-pipe neck at anchor-relative `(40,24,0)`, exposing the original 27-slot packager bidirectionally. Its brass body reaches the boundary at z0 and extends into the sealed housing, seating both ordinary pipe and the thicker wall sleeve without an air gap.

Native validation checks bounds, sprite UVs, closed surfaces, coplanar faces and cross-part seams. A new exported-shell regression probes 81,162 points across all six continuous body boundaries, rejects exposed transport components, and verifies the six-pixel neck at z0. Narrow and shared exports must reproduce the same bytes. The exported JSON/PNG preview uses back-face culling and a depth buffer; it is an offline asset review, not a gameplay screenshot. Power, drone, pad, pickup, door and tower layout assets remain outside this generator's scope. Gameplay validation is reported by the integrating task.
